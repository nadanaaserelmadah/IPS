\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Learned visual--inertial odometry (VIO) regresses the whole relative pose, including parts the camera and IMU already measure. Replacing one pose component at a time by ground truth (an \emph{oracle swap}) shows that the failing component depends on the dataset: on KITTI it is a slowly accumulating rotation bias and then the step length; on EuRoC it is the translation direction, which does not transfer across rooms. On KITTI, a 4\% timing error in how the inertial input is commonly integrated had hidden that the gyroscope removes the rotation error. We propose an \emph{observability gate} that, per edge and per component, uses the sensor measurement where the sensors observe that component and keeps a frozen learned network elsewhere. With one configuration, chosen on held-out data and pre-registered, the gate takes three seeds of a retrained VIFT (3.04\%) to 1.56\% $t_\mathrm{rel}$ on KITTI without test-set checkpoint selection (1.65\% against lidar-SLAM poses independent of the IMU), against VIFT's published 1.96\%; it lowers EuRoC ATE from 5.5--6.6\,m to 0.94--0.99\,m, and, run frozen on a model we did not train, VS-VIO, it lowers KITTI $t_\mathrm{rel}$ from 2.35\% to 1.27\%. On a third dataset, TUM-VI, the pre-registered gate fails because it trusts the network as much as on KITTI. A post-hoc revision that estimates this trust online, without ground truth, removes the TUM-VI failure (there it falls back to the measured pipeline), leaves KITTI and VS-VIO unchanged and improves the two-sequence EuRoC runs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{L}{earned} VIO has moved from end-to-end recurrent models \cite{Clark2017,Han2019} to visual modality selection \cite{Yang2022} and transformer fusion of frozen visual and inertial latents: VIFT \cite{Kurt2024} reports 1.96\% $t_\mathrm{rel}$ on KITTI, and later work reports 1.69--1.77\% \cite{Ma2026,Wan2026}. These models regress rotation and translation jointly, and improvements are sought inside the network: rotation losses \cite{Chen2022}, longer context, new fusion modules.

We first ask \emph{which part of the pose is wrong}. An oracle swap answers this without retraining: replace one predicted component by ground truth and re-score. On KITTI (VIFT's released-code split), ground-truth rotation with the network's own translation already scores 1.55\%, better than VIFT's 1.96\%. A gyroscope measures rotation directly, yet integrating the gyroscope windows that VIFT receives gives 4.62\%, far worse than the network. The reason is a timing detail: the inertial windows used by VS-VIO and VIFT hold 11 samples per image pair, which suggests 10\,ms steps, but they are the raw 100\,Hz readings interpolated over the \emph{actual} image interval, which is $\approx$104\,ms on KITTI. Integrating with the nominal step under-rotates every frame by 4\% and makes an accurate gyroscope look biased; over the true interval it gives 1.70\% with $r_\mathrm{rel}$ 0.18. On EuRoC the same swap points at a different component, the translation direction, which the camera measures geometrically once rotation is known; on TUM-VI the network direction is 40--87$^\circ$ off.

This suggests a design rule: measure each component where the sensors observe it, and learn only the rest. Observability varies along a trajectory (the step length is unobservable at a stop or at constant velocity, the direction at tiny parallax), so the choice must be made per edge.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A component-wise oracle-swap diagnosis of learned VIO, including the KITTI $\approx$104\,ms / +60\,ms timing finding, showing that the failing component is dataset-dependent.
\item An observability gate that chooses between sensor measurements and a frozen network per edge and per component from sensor-computable signals only, pre-registered on KITTI, EuRoC and TUM-VI and run frozen on a model we did not train, with every failure reported; and a post-hoc online estimate of the network's trustworthiness that removes the TUM-VI failure.
\item Measurement components validated against references independent of the IMU (lidar SLAM, motion capture), a protocol audit of VIFT, and an analysis of what remains.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Classical VIO.} Filtering \cite{Mourikis2007} and optimization \cite{Leutenegger2015,Qin2018,Campos2021} integrate the gyroscope between frames with accurate timestamps and calibrated camera--IMU offsets \cite{Furgale2013,Qin2018b}. With rotation known, relative pose reduces to a two-point problem \cite{Kneip2011,Troiani2014}, and metric scale and gravity follow from a linear visual--inertial alignment \cite{Martinelli2014,Qin2017}. We reuse these ingredients inside a learned pipeline.

\textbf{Learned VIO.} VINet \cite{Clark2017}, DeepVIO \cite{Han2019}, SelfVIO \cite{Almalioglu2022}, VS-VIO \cite{Yang2022} and VIFT \cite{Kurt2024} learn the full pose; DB-VIO \cite{Wan2026} injects a gyro-integrated attitude prior into a dual-branch network, MixVIO \cite{Ma2026} selects modalities with phase-aware attention, Diff-VIO \cite{Qin2026} refines learned poses with a diffusion model, and DAP-Pose \cite{DAPPose} aligns modalities in time and adds GNSS. All of them learn components that a sensor measures; we instead decide, per edge, which components to learn at all. Learned inertial odometry \cite{Liu2020,Herath2020} and AI-IMU \cite{Brossard2020} address the inertial side only.

\textbf{Component analysis.} RAUM-VO \cite{Cimarelli2022} substitutes ground-truth rotation and translation into an unsupervised monocular VO network and replaces the learned rotation by an epipolar one at inference. We extend the substitution to learned VIO, to a third component (step length) and to datasets where the failing component is the translation direction. A GNSS-aided pipeline also takes orientation from the gyroscope and translation from learned flow inside a filter \cite{Kang2025}.

\textbf{KITTI: rotation, then length.} With ground-truth rotation the network translation scores 1.55\%. The network's per-frame rotation noise is small (0.014--0.034$^\circ$); the damage is a bias of a few thousandths of a degree per frame; its yaw component changes sign between drives, and constant corrections gain at most 0.13 points, none beating VIFT. A gyroscope should remove it, yet integrating VIFT's inertial windows with the nominal 10\,ms step gives 4.62\%.

\textbf{The timing finding.} We recovered the raw 100\,Hz OXTS stream and image timestamps from the public KITTI raw archives. VIFT's inertial windows equal the raw stream linearly interpolated at ten equal sub-steps of the \emph{true} image interval, which is 103.7--104.1\,ms on drives 04--10. Integrating over the true interval (trapezoidal rule on raw sample times) gives a median per-frame error of 0.023--0.037$^\circ$ and 1.70\% / 0.18 with the network translation. A constant offset between image timestamps and the odometry ground truth, +60\,ms (+62.5\,ms on one recording day), and the IMU-to-camera rotation are fitted on training drives only. KITTI's cameras are triggered by the lidar spin \cite{Geiger2013}; learned-VIO work assumes a nominal 10\,Hz \cite{Yang2022,Kurt2024,Wan2026,Almalioglu2022}, and we found no prior measurement of the 104\,ms period or the 60\,ms offset. With the gyro rotation and the network translation fixed, the next component the swap points at is the step length.

\textbf{EuRoC: direction.} Here ground-truth rotation gives no gain, while ground-truth translation drops ATE below the zero-motion floor on three of four sequences. The learned direction error grows with distance from the training rooms (PI-VIFT: 53$^\circ$ on MH\_05, four training sequences in the same room; 66$^\circ$ on V2\_02, one; 70--90$^\circ$ on V1, none), approaching a random direction. Neither an up-to-scale direction loss \cite{Wang2020} nor unfreezing the encoder made it transfer (one seed each; ATE 6.60 and 3.60\,m). On TUM-VI, where we did not run the swap, the network direction is 40--87$^\circ$ off and its length 14--83\% too long.

\subsection{Rotation from the gyroscope}
We integrate the raw gyroscope over the true image interval, $\Delta\mathbf{R}_k=\prod_i \exp([\bar{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_i-\mathbf{b}_g]_\times\,\Delta t_i)$, with the bias $\mathbf{b}_g$ from a static start, and rotate it into the camera frame with the calibrated extrinsic. Rotation comes from the gyroscope, except inside KITTI OXTS outages longer than 50\,ms, where the network rotation is kept ($\le$0.01\% effect).

\subsection{Direction from two-view geometry}
With $\mathbf{R}_k$ known, each tracked corner pair (bearings $\mathbf{x}_A,\mathbf{x}_B$) gives one linear constraint $\mathbf{t}^\top\big((\mathbf{R}_k\mathbf{x}_B)\times\mathbf{x}_A\big)=0$, so two corners fix the direction. We track corners with forward--backward Lucas--Kanade, undistort them (radial--tangential or equidistant fisheye, limited to 60$^\circ$ off-axis), run two-point RANSAC with a 1\,px epipolar threshold, refit on the inliers by SVD and fix the sign by cheirality. The first-order uncertainty $\sigma_{\mathrm{geo}}$ follows from the singular values; below 2\,px of de-rotated parallax the direction is re-measured over 200 or 300\,ms.

\subsection{Length from the accelerometer}
Over a window of $W$ consecutive edges we solve a linear alignment \cite{Qin2017} for the initial velocity, gravity, accelerometer bias (with a ridge prior) and the $W$ step lengths, given gyro attitudes, preintegrated accelerations and the edge directions. $W$ is 500 edges on KITTI ($\approx$52\,s, chosen on training drives) and 20 on EuRoC and TUM-VI (2\,s). A window is used if it is observable: on KITTI if its design matrix has condition number below $10^4$, on EuRoC and TUM-VI if its gravity estimate lies within 0.5\,m/s$^2$ of 9.81; otherwise the network length is kept.

\subsection{The observability gate}
Rotation is the gyroscope's. For the direction, let $\sigma_g^2=\sigma_{\mathrm{geo}}^2+(A/p)^2$, where $p$ is the de-rotated parallax and $A=6.755^\circ$\,px a floor fitted on KITTI training drives. If $\angle(\dnet,\dgeo)\le 3\sqrt{\sigma_g^2+\sigma_{\mathrm{net}}^2}$, the two are fused with inverse-variance weights; otherwise $\dgeo$ replaces $\dnet$ when the angle exceeds $3\sigma_g$, and $\dnet$ is kept on the remaining edges. Each length window is solved with both $\dnet$ and the gated direction, keeping the solution with the smaller alignment misfit, so a few wrong directions cannot corrupt a window. In the pre-registered version (v3), $\sigma_{\mathrm{net}}=0.83^\circ$, the network's median direction error on held-out KITTI 08. Fig.~\ref{fig:gate} summarizes the decisions.

(b) Length: the accelerometer where its window is observable, the network elsewhere. Rotation always comes from the gyroscope. v3 uses a fixed $\sigma_{\mathrm{net}}=0.83^\circ$; v4 replaces it by the online $\hat\sigma$.}
\label{fig:gate}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Online trust (v4).} A fixed $\sigma_{\mathrm{net}}$ assumes the network is as good everywhere as on KITTI. v4 estimates it causally without ground truth: $\hat\sigma(k)$ is the median angle between $\dnet$ and $\dgeo$ over the last 100 \emph{confident} edges (geometry present, $p\ge5$\,px, $\sigma_g\le1^\circ$), with the 0.83$^\circ$ prior until 20 such edges exist. If $\hat\sigma>\tau=10^\circ$, edges without geometry hold the last gated direction and the length windows use only the gated direction. The confidence constants were fixed from first principles and $\tau$ was selected on held-out data (Sec.~\ref{sec:gate}).

\textbf{Runtime.} The measurement side costs 39--41\,ms per 100\,ms edge on one Apple M1 Pro core (512$\times$512 fisheye frames), 2.5$\times$ faster than real time; the network runs unchanged on the GPU.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Setup and protocol}
\textbf{Data.} KITTI odometry \cite{Geiger2012} (training 00, 01, 02, 04, 06, 09, plus 08 in one split; test 05, 07, 10); EuRoC \cite{Burri2016} (training MH\_01--04 and V2\_01, validation V1\_03, test MH\_05, V2\_02, V1\_01, V1\_02, with the ground-truth quaternion order corrected); TUM-VI \cite{Schubert2018} (training room1--4, test room5--6; 16-bit frames converted to 8 bits, as a naive conversion saturates every frame).
\textbf{Networks.} KITTI: VIFT retrained with our recipe (angle-weighted L1), 3 seeds per split, with mirror augmentation on the clean split. EuRoC: VIFT-style and PI-VIFT (VIFT with preintegration tokens), 3 seeds each; the 4-sequence gate runs use PI-VIFT seed 123 and a single-seed PI-VIFT trained with a direction loss. TUM-VI: PI-VIFT fine-tuned from the EuRoC checkpoints, 3 seeds. Cross-model: VS-VIO \cite{Yang2022} with its released weights (trained with 08).
\textbf{Metrics and references.} KITTI $t_\mathrm{rel}$ and $r_\mathrm{rel}$ over 100--800\,m segments, against the OXTS ground truth and against SuMa lidar-SLAM poses \cite{Behley2018}, which do not use the IMU; EuRoC and TUM-VI SE(3) ATE on the 10\,Hz edge chain, next to the zero-motion floor.
\textbf{Protocol.} VIFT's released code trains on 08 although its paper excludes it, and selects checkpoints on the test drives. Our headline (\emph{clean}) protocol uses the paper's split and selects on held-out 08; the released-code split, with checkpoints selected on the test drives as in VIFT's code, is reported as secondary. Gate constants were fixed on training or held-out data and the success criterion was registered before a single test run: match or beat the better hand-designed pipeline within 0.05 $t_\mathrm{rel}$ (KITTI) or 0.1\,m (EuRoC, TUM-VI). One deviation: the registered text required both length checks of Sec.~\ref{sec:method} on every dataset, while the code that produced all results applies one per dataset.

\textbf{Pre-registered result (v3).} Table~\ref{tab:gate}: without being told the dataset, the gate meets the criterion on KITTI and on EuRoC's 4-sequence means, and, run frozen on VS-VIO, beats the hand-designed chain there. On the two-sequence EuRoC runs, 3 of 5 miss the 0.1\,m tolerance. On TUM-VI it fails: 0.69\,m mean over three seeds against a limit of 0.57\,m, most of the loss on room5. Before v3, the pre-registered v1 (a constant floor in degrees) and a post-hoc v2 (a pixel floor coupled into the length solve) had failed on KITTI, and those test results informed the diagnosis; v3 itself was designed only on held-out 08.

\textbf{Why it fails, and v4.} $\sigma_{\mathrm{net}}=0.83^\circ$ describes the network on KITTI; on TUM-VI the network is tens of degrees off, so wherever it happens to agree with geometry the fusion over-weights it, and edges without geometry inherit its direction. v4 replaces $\sigma_{\mathrm{net}}$ by the online estimate of Sec.~\ref{sec:method}. It was designed only on held-out data: KITTI 08 and zero-shot predictions on TUM-VI room1--4. There $\hat\sigma$ recovers the KITTI value without ground truth (0.77--0.80$^\circ$ against 0.83$^\circ$) and flags the TUM-VI network as unreliable (25--74$^\circ$); the pre-declared rule selected $\tau=10^\circ$. Run once on every test set, v4 equals v3 where the network is good (KITTI, VS-VIO; $\hat\sigma\approx0.7^\circ$), fixes the two-sequence EuRoC runs (0.57--0.59 against 0.62 hand-designed) and reaches 0.47\,m on all TUM-VI seeds. There it equals the network-free pipeline: for a network that is tens of degrees off the gate stops doing harm, but the network adds nothing. Because v4 was motivated by a test failure and the KITTI and EuRoC test sequences had been used in earlier gate runs, we label it post-hoc.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

Fig.~\ref{fig:observ} summarizes the rule: the useful split between measured and learned components follows observability and differs between datasets, and the trust placed in the learned part should itself be measured, not assumed. A cheap oracle swap tells which component to fix first; on KITTI it avoids searching for better rotation losses.

\textbf{Limitations.} (i) KITTI's IMU and ground truth share the OXTS unit; SuMa and an OXTS-free calibration bound this effect (+0.1 point in $t_\mathrm{rel}$; $r_\mathrm{rel}$ 0.18$\to$0.32), and TUM-VI motion capture validates the estimators independently. (ii) v4 is post-hoc and needs confirmation on data unseen at every stage. (iii) The gate cannot beat its measurements: where neither geometry nor the network is reliable (TUM-VI room5's sub-3\,cm steps) nothing helps. (iv) The length check differs between datasets as implemented (condition number on KITTI, gravity norm on EuRoC/TUM-VI), and the pixel constants were not rescaled per camera. (v) Frame-to-frame estimation without windowed optimization remains about 25$\times$ behind ORB-SLAM3 run by us and 7$\times$ behind VINS-Mono as reported. (vi) The gyro bias needs a static start; an online visual estimate is the obvious next step.

\section{Conclusion}

Learned VIO fails in identifiable, dataset-dependent places, and an oracle swap finds them. Measuring what the sensors observe, keeping the learned network only for the rest, and measuring how far to trust it yields a single configuration that improves VIFT to 1.56\% on KITTI without test-set selection, brings EuRoC from 5.5--6.6\,m to below 1\,m, improves a model we did not train, and, once its trust is estimated online (post-hoc), stops doing harm on a third dataset. Natural next steps are networks that predict only the unobservable components together with their uncertainty, and windowed optimization over the gated estimates.
